# Scientific review: implementation, evidence and open questions

Reviewed 29 October 2026 (Asia/Dhaka). This is a source and committed-export review, not a rerun of scientific training or source downloads. The figures below describe the checked-in results, not a fresh estimate of model performance. Current software verification is recorded in [VERIFICATION.md](VERIFICATION.md).

## What the project actually does

Agun Panjika builds a weekly Bangladesh fire-activity record on the VIIRS Suomi-NPP detection scale. It estimates the pre-VIIRS record from MODIS, uses filtered S-NPP observations where available, and includes successor-satellite continuity logic. The result supports historical comparisons, calendar views, seasonal percentiles, maps, exploratory short-term outlooks and cross-border comparisons. It estimates satellite detections, not numbers of unique fire events, burned hectares, fire intensity or ground-truth wildfire incidence. Sources: [pipeline orchestration](../pipeline/run_all.py#L36), [harmonized record construction](../pipeline/climatology.py#L65), [exports](../pipeline/export.py#L90).

The current exported Bangladesh record starts in ISO 2001-W01 and ends in 2026-W41. The manifest was generated on 10 October 2026 and reports all four sensors through 9 October 2026. The national file contains 1,345 weeks: 576 estimated and 769 observed, with no bridged weeks yet. The exported baseline is 2003–2025. There are five analysis regions and 64 district documents. Sources: [manifest](../web/public/data/manifest.json), [national](../web/public/data/national.json).

These timestamps matter: as of this review, the checked-in files are not current live observations. The fire-weather file was generated on 10 October and its forecast ends on 25 October. The two-week outlook was issued at 2026-W41. The cross-border Dhaka AOD series ends at 2026-W27. Sources: [weather](../web/public/data/fireweather.json), [outlook](../web/public/data/forecast.json), [neighbours](../web/public/data/neighbours.json).

## Results that can be quoted, with their scope

The committed [validation results](../web/public/data/validation.json) use leave-one-year-out comparison over 2012–2025 and evaluate national weekly detection counts against S-NPP. The table reports averages across held-out years:

| Model | Weekly MAE | Off-season weekly MAE | Mean absolute annual-total percentage error |
| --- | ---: | ---: | ---: |
| Fixed 4.4x | 60.971 | 26.095 | 20.150% |
| Single factor fitted within fold | 49.450 | 28.705 | 12.785% |
| Monthly factors | 43.180 | 21.933 | 11.888% |
| Negative binomial | 44.110 | 19.458 | 9.652% |
| LightGBM | 38.684 | 15.347 | 10.426% |
| Shipped ensemble | 36.964 | 15.861 | 9.501% |

Scope of the result: “Our current internal leave-one-year-out evaluation reports about 25% lower national weekly MAE than a single fitted scaling factor.” This is 25.25% relative error reduction, not “25% more accurate,” not a 95% accuracy claim, and not a prospective deployment result. The ensemble's reduction against fixed 4.4x is 39.374%. It reduces off-season MAE by 44.745% versus the fitted factor and beats that baseline in all 14 evaluated off-seasons, but LightGBM alone has the lower overall off-season MAE. Source: [metric definitions](../pipeline/harmonise.py#L415), [summary aggregation](../pipeline/harmonise.py#L443), [JSON](../web/public/data/validation.json).

Empirical 80% interval coverage is 80.1% region-week, 81.3% national-week, and 85.7% region-year/national-year. Median national weekly interval width is 1.39 times its estimate, so the uncertainty is often substantial. Terra-only weekly MAE is 66.76 versus 99.58 for a single Terra factor; mean annual error 17.87% versus 24.11%. Source: [validation JSON](../web/public/data/validation.json).

In the [bridge export](../web/public/data/bridge.json), NOAA-20 uses 365 overlap weeks from 2019–2025; identity gives 4.58% mean absolute held-out total error and weekly MAE 32.44. NOAA-21 uses 143 weeks from 2024–2026, including partial years; identity gives 10.79% and 38.09. Both single/monthly corrections scored worse. “Continuity bridge implemented and evaluated” is fair; “both successors perfectly interchangeable in every district/season” is not supported.

The [ESA cross-check](../web/public/data/partner.json) reports post-/pre-2012 median detection-to-burned-area ratio 0.95 for harmonized series versus 3.09 for raw stitched counts. Log annual correlation with burned area is 0.79 versus 0.00 for stitched counts; estimated-years-only correlation 0.93; seasonal cycle correlation 0.98. The ratio is one aggregate diagnostic, not proof that all sensor bias is removed. The ESA product changes in 2019, another comparability issue worth testing.

The [forecast export](../web/public/data/forecast.json) reports 6,924 pooled national/region issue weeks over hindcast years 2003–2025. Two-week Brier skill is 0.110 and ROC AUC 0.708 using fire history alone; with observed target-week FWI standing in for a perfect forecast, 0.213 and 0.768. This second result is an optimistic experiment, not measured weather-forecast performance. At probability >=0.5, the exported full-model hit rate is 37.5% and false-alarm ratio 32.5%; sample intervals calibrated on the same pooled hindcasts have 80.1% in-sample coverage. Remainder-of-season full-model BSS is -0.001, fire-only 0.040; `has_skill` is false. Sources: [features](../pipeline/forecast.py#L166), [evaluation](../pipeline/forecast.py#L238), [interval calibration](../pipeline/forecast.py#L264).

## Important limitations and improvement opportunities

These are review findings and proposed work, not a claim that the team already committed to or completed fixes.

1. **Separate retrospective reconstruction from prospective forecasting.** Harmonization uses next week's MODIS count (`lM_next` and Terra equivalent), appropriate for retrospective reconstruction but unavailable at issue time. Labels such as “real-time harmonization” need qualification. In addition, forecast hindcasts consume a single already-harmonized record trained using all overlap years; a strict end-to-end temporal evaluation should refit learned preprocessing/reconstruction within each split. [Features](../pipeline/harmonise.py#L59), [forecast inputs](../pipeline/forecast.py#L64).
2. **Make the uncertainty assessment strictly nested before calling it nested validation.** `interval_coverage` excludes year y's residuals from calibration, but the other LOYO residuals were produced by models that can include y in their training. The function does not rerun inner model training with y excluded from every inner fit. This limits the strength of “fully independent nested” or guaranteed-coverage claims. [LOYO predictions](../pipeline/harmonise.py#L431), [interval coverage](../pipeline/harmonise.py#L495).
3. **Protect model selection and static-filter evaluation from reuse of test data.** The shipped ensemble weight is fixed in code, and the reviewed files do not document an independent final holdout used after model/feature selection. Static-source cells are learned on all detections before evaluation. Manual spot-check labels were used to adjust heuristics; a new blind sample, confidence intervals and retained-fire sensitivity are needed for an independent filter assessment. [Shipped weights](../pipeline/harmonise.py#L49), [filter](../pipeline/kilns.py#L29), [spot checks](../docs/kiln_spotcheck_round3.csv).
4. **Test handover/outage behavior across all geographic products.** The main record uses month-specific factors, but `local.unit_series` averages nonzero successor counts directly and does not implement its docstring's pre-handover S-NPP outage fallback. `neighbours.series` similarly uses uncorrected successors and does not force switching at the configured handover. Identity factors happen to be selected now, but future nonidentity selection exposes divergent behavior. Main `unit_json` omits bridge interval bounds despite the underlying table computing them; `write_points` chooses the sensor per year rather than per handover week. Validate mixed-sensor boundary weeks, real zero counts, absent coverage and district/local consistency before operational use. [Local series](../pipeline/local.py#L93), [neighbours](../pipeline/neighbours.py#L182), [main bridge](../pipeline/climatology.py#L65), [JSON](../pipeline/export.py#L90), [points](../pipeline/export.py#L187).
5. **Validate actual issue-time forecasts.** Archive weather forecasts and evaluate rolling forward in time, including POWER latency, missing forecasts, calibration and warning tradeoffs. “Live performance is guaranteed to lie between fire-only and perfect-FWI results” is not a demonstrated mathematical bound; call these sensitivity experiments. Calibrate forecast intervals on separate data; the exported interval metric is explicitly in-sample. [Forecast docstring](../pipeline/forecast.py#L1), [hindcast](../pipeline/forecast.py#L206), [coverage export](../pipeline/forecast.py#L289).
6. **Resolve freshness and provenance before sharing a live link.** Add/check sensor coverage completeness, export version, source timestamps, missing-data behavior and stale outlook handling. Missing API chunks can be skipped; aggregation then zero-fills within the overall available sensor span. A successful build does not alone prove complete upstream data. [API failure handling](../pipeline/ingest.py#L193), [zero filling](../pipeline/aggregate.py#L33), [manifest](../pipeline/export.py#L225).
7. **Demonstrate transfer and practical value.** Run an independently validated second-country example and a small real-user evaluation focused on interpreting sensor changes, uncertainty and calendar tasks. The Nepal configuration, older CHT boundaries and translation dictionaries establish implementation intent, not validated international adoption. [Country paths](../pipeline/config.py#L26), [local names/boundaries](../pipeline/local.py#L27).

## Manual static-source evidence

The three CSVs each contain 20 manually labeled locations: round 1 = 12 Y, 7 N, 1 unsure; round 2 = 15 Y, 1 N, 4 unsure; round 3 = 16 Y, 2 N, 2 unsure. Round 3 therefore has 80% explicitly positive labels, but it follows heuristic tuning and includes at least one location already present in round 1. It is not an independent large precision study or proof of recall. The current static-cell export contains 124 cells; national `static_removed` is 15,684 detections summed across non-MODIS sensors, so it is not the number of kilns or unique fires. Sources: [round 1](kiln_spotcheck_round1.csv), [round 2](kiln_spotcheck_round2.csv), [round 3](kiln_spotcheck_round3.csv), [static-cell export](../web/public/data/static_cells.json), [removal calculation](../pipeline/export.py#L260).

## Tests and read-only checks

All three Python test files were reviewed. Unit tests cover normalization, synthetic static cells versus daytime/jhum fire, NB coefficient recovery, seasonal model behavior, interval shapes, FWI reference/dry-rain behavior and synthetic conformal coverage. Export checks cover intervals, schema, regional/district sums, historical start/unfiltered totals, forecast outputs, ESA comparison, bilingual feeds and expected older-boundary CHT upazila counts. Contrast tests cover theme tokens. Sources: [pipeline tests](../tests/test_pipeline.py), [warning tests](../tests/test_warning.py), [contrast tests](../tests/test_contrast.py).

Many scientific export assertions in `test_pipeline.py` are collectively gated on `data/processed/detections.parquet`, even where their inputs are committed JSON. Therefore a green suite with skipped raw-data tests is not a complete scientific validation rerun. [Gate](../tests/test_pipeline.py#L23).

A read-only check compared all 1,345 exported national weeks against the five region and 64 district JSON sums using the Python standard library. Maximum discrepancies from rounding were 0.2 detections for region sums and 0.9 for district sums. Counts of local units, static cells and manual labels above were also computed directly. No scientific data, trained models or application code was changed.

Coverage: all 16 substantive pipeline modules (`ingest`, `config`, `regions`, `kilns`, `aggregate`, `weather`, `fireweather`, `harmonise`, `bridge`, `climatology`, `local`, `partner`, `forecast`, `neighbours`, `export`, `run_all`), package init, both country configurations, region schema, all Python test files, scientific summary exports, national record, every region/district weekly series for additivity, local export counts and all three spot-check CSVs. Notebook figure entrypoints were identified; publication figures were not reproduced.
