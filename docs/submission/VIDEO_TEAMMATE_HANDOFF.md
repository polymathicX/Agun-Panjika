# Agun Panjika: video teammate brief

**Team KUETaguns | Final product pitch | Aim for 29 seconds**

Tell a simple story: **understand the fire story where you live.** Show the finished app doing useful things. Let the globe, the seasonal comparison and the phone experience carry the explanation. Use the seven-slide deck as the visual reference.

## What people get

Agun Panjika combines recent NASA fire observations worldwide with historical fire calendars. MODIS and VIIRS see heat at different resolutions; we connect their records so seasonal patterns are easier to compare.

- **Choose your country:** spin, zoom and tap the Earth. Explore recent MODIS, NOAA-20 or NOAA-21 observations in 242 countries and territories.
- **Understand the numbers:** change the satellite or day and read a short explanation tied to that selection. Open its basis and limits whenever needed.
- **Explore the seasons:** choose Bangladesh, Nepal, Chin or Punjab–Haryana; reveal VIIRS and compare the reconstructed history with satellite observations.
- **Learn at your pace:** an eight-step introduction and 85 on-demand help topics cover all nine pages, in English and Bangla.
- **Keep it close:** save countries on the device, install the PWA, revisit cached reference pages and share a dated brief.

The human connection: a student can investigate their country's pattern; a community can share a view with its source attached; researchers can inspect seasons worth studying.

## Style and final check

Keep Soft Earth colors: warm paper, deep blue, coral. Use large words, clear spacing and real interactions. Explain one idea per shot. Let a viewer see the action before the next cut. Keep music low enough for a natural voice and captions readable on a phone.

Use **satellite detections** for the numbers and **historical reconstruction** for the corrected record. Worldwide recent observations and four historical areas are different scopes. Present learning, sharing and monitoring as intended benefits. The plain readings explain the published data; they do not issue safety decisions.

Export **1920×1080 H.264 MP4, at most 30 seconds**, with English captions. Keep NASA FIRMS/LANCE/ESDIS credit, imagery credits and a visible assistance acknowledgment. Suggested line: “AI-assisted development and editing; actual NASA observations and app recordings.”
